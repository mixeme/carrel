Key escrow

Key escrow is not a key backup. It is a way in: an administrator holding the master password can decrypt a covered user's saved DAV credentials, and through them their contacts and calendars. Every covered user is told so in their profile, and every recovery is logged and mailed to them.

{{if $e.Configured}}

{{if $e.Enabled}}New accounts are covered.{{else}}New accounts are not covered; copies taken earlier stay recoverable.{{end}} Accounts the master password can open: {{$d.EscrowCoverage}}.

{{if $e.Enabled}}

Turning deposit off stops covering new accounts. Copies already taken are left alone — deleting one is its owner's decision, from their profile.

{{else}}

Required so that deposit is not switched back on by someone who can no longer recover anything.

{{end}}

Users see this policy in their profile whether or not it currently affects them.

Only the recovery private key is re-encrypted. The deposited copies are untouched, so nobody has to opt in again.

{{else}}

Choose a master password that is not your login password and keep it somewhere outside this service. If it is lost the scheme becomes useless: the deposited copies can never be opened again, and generating a new key pair would orphan them.

At least 16 characters. Escrow will apply to accounts created from now on; everyone else may join from their own profile.

{{end}}
{{if $e.Configured}}

Recover an account

The master password opens the deposited copy of the user's data key and re-wraps it under a temporary password you choose. Their connections survive. The user is mailed a notice of it, which is not optional.

Hand it over in person. They must change it at their next sign-in.

{{end}}

Reset a password

This is destructive. The user's data key is replaced, so every connection they saved becomes unreadable and is deleted. Nothing about it can be undone. For an account covered by key escrow, recover it instead — that keeps the data.
